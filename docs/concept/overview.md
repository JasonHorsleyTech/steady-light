# Game Concept Overview

## Elevator Pitch

A coming-of-age RPG where the game systems themselves lie to you the way the world lies to children — and growing up means seeing through the mechanics, not just the story.

## The Experience

The player starts as a child and grows up. The game presents itself as a standard, even boring, RPG with familiar systems: health bars, stat allocation, quests, NPCs giving advice. The player is meant to feel comfortable, maybe even a little bored by how generic it all seems.

Then the rug gets pulled.

The systems the player has been trusting — the stats they allocated, the advice NPCs gave, the "correct" quest paths — were all built on the same flawed assumptions that real life hands you as a kid. Growing up in the game means the same thing it means in life: realizing that the framework you were given was incomplete, biased, or wrong, and having to rebuild your understanding while still moving forward.

## Design Principles

1. **Show, don't tell.** The player should never be lectured. They should discover the philosophy by bumping into it through gameplay friction.
2. **Familiarity is the weapon.** The more generic and RPG-standard something looks, the more effectively it can be subverted.
3. **Boring is a feature.** Comfort and security come from boredom. The player needs to feel safe before you can meaningfully challenge them.
4. **Mechanics are the message.** The stat system, the economy, the quest structure — these ARE the philosophical delivery mechanism, not the dialogue.
5. **No moral high ground.** The game isn't smarter than the player. It's recreating a universal experience: growing up and realizing the world is more complicated than you were told.

## Target Feeling

The player should feel what it felt like to be 22 and realize that the confident advice your parents gave you at 16 was wrong — not because they were malicious, but because they didn't know either. That specific mix of betrayal, empathy, and determination to figure it out yourself.
