# Player Psychology & Subversion Strategy

## The Spec Ops Playbook

Spec Ops: The Line worked because it used the player's familiarity with military shooters against them. They did terrible things because the game told them to and they were used to following game instructions. Then it asked: why did you keep playing?

Steady Light does the same thing with RPG conventions:
- Stat allocation that feels empowering but is actually self-deception
- NPC advice that feels helpful but is actually incomplete/wrong
- Quest paths that feel "correct" but are actually the marketed/sold path
- Familiar systems that breed complacency

## Comfort → Subversion Pipeline

1. **Present familiar systems.** Red bar, green bar, blue bar. Stat points. NPCs with exclamation marks. Quest log.
2. **Let the player settle in.** They should feel like they've played this game before. Good. That's the point.
3. **Create friction.** Something doesn't work the way they expect. A stat check fails when it shouldn't. An NPC's advice leads to a bad outcome.
4. **Reveal the truth.** The system was never what they thought. The revelation should feel earned, not cheap.
5. **Force adaptation.** Now they have to play with the real rules, and they can't unsee what they've seen.

## What The Player Should NOT Feel

- Lectured at
- Morally judged
- Like the game is smarter than them
- Punished for trusting the systems (the whole point is that trusting was the right move at the time)

## What The Player SHOULD Feel

- The specific vertigo of realizing a framework you trusted was incomplete
- Empathy for the people who gave them the incomplete framework
- Determination to figure out the real rules
- That growing up is not about rejecting what you were taught, but about building on it with new understanding
