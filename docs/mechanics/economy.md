# Economy & False Progression

## The Treadmill

The small town economy is designed to feel like progress while actually being a trap. Every system — shops, guild, bank, bar — is built to extract silver and keep the player in a break-even loop. The NPCs aren't malicious; they're just professionals at sales, not dragon-slaying.

---

## Daily Economy (Book 1 — Small Town)

### Income
| Source | Pay | Availability |
|--------|-----|-------------|
| Farm chores (eggs, milking) | 1 silver/day total | Daily, guaranteed |
| Slime clearing (farmer's field) | 1 silver per slime | Daily |
| Side jobs (neighbor farms) | 1 silver | 25% spawn chance/day |

### Expenses
| Cost | Amount | Notes |
|------|--------|-------|
| Barn rent | 3 silver/day | Auto-drafted |
| Bar drink | 1 silver/night | "Restores energy" — blocks growth |
| Fighters Guild membership | 1 silver/day | Teaches slime-only moves |
| Bank account fee | 5 silver/week | Fine print |
| Bank overdraft penalty | 10 silver | Compounding trap |

### Net Effect
A typical day: earn 2-3 silver from chores + slimes. Spend 3 on rent. If you drink, spend 4. If you're in the guild, 5. Breaking even feels like an achievement. You are running in place.

## The Shops

### Weaponsmith
- 2-3 tiers: basic stick → "premium" sword → "elite" blade
- Prices escalate. Damage increase: +10%, +20%, +50% over base
- **Reality:** Slimes die in 3 hits with a stick. "Premium" sword makes it 2.7 hits. Marketing copy: "Can end combat in up to five fewer turns!"
- The copy exaggerates. Raw numbers are never shown.

### Armorer
- Similar tier structure. Incremental damage reduction
- **Reality:** Slimes barely do damage. Armor is negligible
- Heavy armor costs 2 MP/move (slows you in combat)
- Light armor costs 1 MP/move

### Fighters Guild
- Teaches "special techniques" — all are 10% more effective only vs. slimes
- Core combat advice is truthy but incomplete: "If low on energy, step back and recover"
- Omits system nuances (stat separation, rhythm timing, the real ways to grow)

---

## The False Progression Loop

1. Player arrives in town with nothing
2. Farmer offers barn + work (immediate relief)
3. NPCs point to shops and guild as "the next step"
4. Player grinds, buys gear, joins guild
5. Marginal improvement against slimes (which were already easy)
6. Money drains to rent, drinks, fees
7. Player is slightly better at fighting slimes and slightly poorer
8. Repeat

**The intended realization:** Following town advice burns cash without changing your real ability to win against anything outside the slime bubble. Merchants and guilds are professionals at keeping you coming back, not at preparing you for the world.

---

## The Real Progression

Hidden behind the treadmill:
- **Combat mastery** through stat growth (landing attacks, blocking, dodging)
- **Self-study** at the library (discovering non-local skills)
- **Refusing the drink** to unlock reflection and stat flips
- **Experimenting** with combat mechanics beyond what the guild teaches
- **Leaving** the safety of the slime economy entirely

The moment the player realizes "any functional gear works if used tactically" is the progression paradigm shift.

---

## Book 2 Economy Shift

Outside the small town:
- Higher costs: ferries, tolls, camp fees
- Scarcer work, better-paying jobs require proof of real combat ability
- Resource management: rations, repair kits, travel gear
- Gear breaks, food spoils, weather slows travel
- Old solutions don't work
- Merchants price-gouge outsiders

The temptation to retreat to the barn is real and intentional.
