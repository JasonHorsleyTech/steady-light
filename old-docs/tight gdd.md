# Game Design Document: Steady Light - Book 1 & Book 2 Focus

## Overview
Steady Light is a small, reflective RPG where you begin in a **small town full of confident incompetents**. The combat, economy, and advice you're given all seem straightforward at first-but almost all of it is an oversimplification. The core player journey in Books 1 & 2 is realizing that the systems you've been taught only work in narrow, local contexts, and that real growth comes from **experimenting beyond bad teachers** and surviving the consequences.

---

## Book 1 - Small Town: Don't Be an NPC
**Core Belief:** Progress requires moving past the limits of others' knowledge, even when you feel unprepared.

### Player Loop
- You land in town with no skills, no gear, no place to sleep.
- A farmer offers barn lodging if you clear his green slimes and do chores (eggs, milking cows). Pay is minimal.
- You're told you can:
  - Get better gear from the weaponsmith (cheap → expensive tiers = +10% to +50% damage).
  - Learn special moves from the Fighters Guild (bonus only vs. local enemy types).
  - Buy armor from the armorer (incremental % reductions vs. enemy types).
- **All advice is correct for local fights**-and completely useless for anything tougher.
- Rent, drinks, and memberships bleed your account. Bank fees lurk in the fine print. Breaking even feels like progress, but it isn't.

### Economy
- **Farmer's Barn:** 3 silver/day auto-drafted.
- **Bar Drink:** 1 silver/night (habit-forming "restore").
- **Fighters Guild:** 1 silver/day for minor, enemy-specific moves.
- **Bank:** 5 silver/week fee; 10 silver overdraft penalty.
- **Side Jobs:** Occasional slime-clearing from other farms (1 silver, 25% spawn chance).

**Goal:** Survive long enough to realize the loop is a trap. The true way forward is:
1. Recognizing bad training has hit its ceiling.
2. Experimenting with self-discovery (library, self-drills, real fights).
3. Taking risks that reveal hidden mechanics.

## Book 2 - Leaving the Loop: The Answer is Not the Cabin
**Core Belief:** Isolation and hoarding skills aren't the answer; real progress requires connection and adaptation.

### Player Loop
- First ventures outside town-wilds, roadside stops, other tiny settlements.
- Encounters quickly outclass your slime-optimized loadout.
- Gear breaks, food spoils, weather slows travel. Old "solutions" no longer work.
- You meet:
  - Travelers with contradictory advice.
  - Fighters who openly mock your "guild" skills.
  - Merchants who price-gouge outsiders.
- Temptation to retreat to the comfort of the barn and its predictable loop is strong-but doing so halts growth.

### Economy Shift
- Higher travel costs (ferries, tolls, camp fees).
- Scarcer work; better-paying jobs require proof you can fight more than slimes.
- Resource management becomes essential (rations, repair kits, travel gear).

**Goal:** Push past the urge to isolate; integrate with new communities and learn from those who've survived more than you.

---

## Stats & Progression in Books 1-2
- **Drive** (energy to act): Grows from taking risks, shrinks when stuck in easy loops.
- **Insight** (understanding systems): Grows from failed experiments and observation, not from NPC training.
- **Stability** (resilience under stress): Grows from balancing needs over time, avoiding burnout.

Each stat has a **false early name** (Calm, Confidence, Passion) and flips after a 3-night arc once you've actually earned it.

---

## Combat in Books 1-2
- **Early:** White-grid, single-bar, turn-based fights. Local enemies (slimes) are slow, predictable.
- **Training:** Moves taught are tied to slime rhythm; bonuses don't apply elsewhere.
- **Evolving:** First real fights outside town punish bad habits (overcommitting, not reading enemy intent).
- **Music tie-in:** One bar = one turn cycle; later fights introduce new time signatures.

---

## Writing Tone
- Present-tense, 4-18 words, grounded physical imagery.
- NPCs speak with conviction but reveal ignorance through specifics.
- Third encounters with systems = flip moments (player sees the con/limit).
- Avoid stat names in dialogue; use lived experience to hint at growth.

---

## Art & Music Direction
**Art:**
- Low-signal pixel art, muted rural palette + 2 accent highlights.
- Clear silhouettes; early combat maps abstract; overworld more detailed later.

**Music:**
- Early: slow, even meter (4/4, 84-96 BPM), soft metronome in combat.
- "Flip" moments marked by tonal shift and sparse instrumentation.
- Later: introduce odd meters as fights get more complex.

---

## Deliverables for Book 1 & 2 Build
- Town map (farmer, guild, smith, armorer, bank, bar).
- 3-night flip arc for each stat.
- Combat reducer with rhythm integration.
- Economy simulation (daily auto-payments, random farm jobs, bank penalties).
- Early wilds maps with 2-3 enemy types outside slime range.
- Library/self-study mechanic for discovering non-local skills.
