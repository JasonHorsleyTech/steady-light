## Progression Paradigm Shift — "You Don’t Need the Best, Just *Some*"

### Context Assumed
Early-game feels like a small-town RPG loop: armorsmith, weaponsmith, Fighter’s Guild, each promising “the next step” toward greatness. In truth, they’re selling *perceived* progress — minor boosts dressed as life-changing upgrades.

---

### What to Do Now
**Set up the false loop:**
- **Shops & Guild:** Each offers 2–3 clear categories (weapon, armor, training), each with a *basic* and a *“premium”* tier.
- **Item Descriptions:** Marketing copy exaggerates impact (“Can end combat in up to five fewer turns!”) instead of raw numbers.
- **Small Gains:** Premiums add marginal benefits (≈10–15%) — mathematically negligible against early enemies.
- **Training:** Guild teaches “core” combat advice (truthy but incomplete), e.g. *“If low on energy, step back and recover”*, omitting system nuances.

---

### What to Do Next
**Bake in the flip:**
- **Realization Trigger:** By mid-game, player notices the “better” gear is mostly fluff — minor stats that vanish in relevance once core stats grow.
- **Mechanics Behind the Curtain:**
  - *Weapons:* Short = 1 beat attack, fast; Heavy = 2 beat attack, ≈double damage. That’s it.
  - *Armor:* Heavy = costs 2 MP/move; Light = 1 MP/move. Heavy good vs. rushers, Light good vs. runners.
  - *Energy Recovery:* “Do nothing” = +1 to lowest stat (Drive/Insight/Stability) per turn → effectively ⅓ “energy” point per turn.
- **True Progress:** Learn situational tactics, not buy bigger numbers.
- **Meta-Town Economy:** Merchants & guilds are professionals at sales, not dragon-slaying — their goal is to keep you coming back.

---

### Success Criteria
- Player starts by chasing “best” gear, training upgrades, and guild ranks.
- Player eventually realizes:
  1. Any functional gear works if used tactically.
  2. Core combat mastery > incremental stat bumps.
  3. Shops & guilds are part of the *false progression loop*.
- Post-flip, player invests time in real growth: mastering beat timings, enemy psychology, and terrain — not spending gold on “uranium clubs” and “glass armor.”
