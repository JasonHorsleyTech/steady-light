Gotcha — so you basically want a false progression loop that feels like classic RPG advice and town economy, but under the hood is intentionally misaligned with real skill growth.

The whole “go buy better gear, train with the guild, get the strongest armor” is actually a treadmill designed for a closed ecosystem (slime-only economy) that doesn’t prepare you for the bigger world. It’s like the in-game version of buying a 5-star-rated $16 “personal alarm” — technically works, but only in the narrowest, least-threatening scenario.

Here’s how I’d lay it out cleanly so it supports your core stat-driven combat paradigm:

⸻

False Progression Layer

The Town Economy
	•	Core loop:
	1.	Sleep in barn (4 silver rent)
	2.	Kill 1–3 green slimes on the farm (+1 silver each, sometimes extra if RNG “neighbor farm” spawns one)
	3.	Do 1–2 farm chores (fixed 1 silver total)
	4.	Optional: drink at bar (1 silver) → raises “morale” stat, but morale isn’t used in any meaningful way early game.
	5.	Bank balance usually hovers at 0–2 silver unless you grind multiple in-game weeks.
	•	NPC advice:
	•	Weaponsmith: “Better sword = stronger hits.” Reality: +10%, +20%, +50% damage tiers, but slimes already die in 3 hits.
	•	Armorer: “Armor keeps you alive.” Reality: Slimes barely do damage; armor effect negligible.
	•	Fighters Guild: Teaches “special techniques” — all are 10% more effective only vs. slimes.
	•	Net effect:
Following town advice burns cash without changing your real ability to win against anything outside the slime bubble.

⸻

Hidden Real Progression Layer

Combat Mastery
The real progression system is entirely tied to your Drive, Insight, and Stability stats:
	•	Drive: Movement freedom — eventually lets you leave the 8×8 combat grid and reposition fights in real-world terrain.
	•	Insight: Awareness — removes “abstract white grid” and keeps real environment visible during combat.
	•	Stability: Technical mastery — lets you move + act in the same beat.

How to actually level them:
	•	Drive: Land clean attacks (not blocked/dodged).
	•	Insight: Successfully block attacks.
	•	Stability: Successfully dodge attacks.

How to lose them:
	•	Drive ↓ when hit.
	•	Insight ↓ when your attack is blocked.
	•	Stability ↓ when you whiff into empty space.

⸻

The Player Realization Arc
	1.	Phase 1 – Belief in the treadmill:
Player follows NPC advice, spends all their silver, gets “better” gear, and still has the same fight experience.
	2.	Phase 2 – Doubt creeps in:
They notice slimes are predictable, and gear changes very little. They experiment mid-combat with dodges, blocks, and counters — accidentally improving core stats.
	3.	Phase 3 – The paradigm shift:
A small stat threshold is crossed (e.g., Drive unlocks real-world movement). Player can now:
	•	Run fights into complex terrain.
	•	Avoid bad matchups.
	•	Use environment for cover.
This is the “Ohhhhhh” moment — real power comes from combat mastery, not town economy.
	4.	Phase 4 – Leaving the bubble:
When they finally leave the slime fields, the first real enemy punishes bad stat balance brutally, forcing them to use the skills they accidentally learned.

Why This Works
	•	The false progression gives the familiar “RPG loop” hook.
	•	The hidden progression ensures the real growth is mechanical skill + stat synergy.
	•	The contrast between them makes the eventual breakthrough feel earned — like the player outsmarted the system.
	•	You also get that meta commentary on bad advice, false economies, and treadmill systems (both in games and life).
